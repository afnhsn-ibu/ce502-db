## 1. Why this assignment

Lesson 1-1 described six careers in database technologies and showed which weeks of CE 502 each one needs most. This assignment turns that discussion into a short piece of research. Instead of describing a role from general knowledge, you will read what employers ask for **today**, in real job adverts, and connect it to the course and to your own plans.

By the end you should be able to:

- describe one database role from evidence, not from memory;
- name the technical skills, soft skills and certifications that employers actually list for it;
- show where the topics of CE 502 appear in that role;
- plan the next twelve months of your own learning.

This assignment checks objective **O4 of Lesson 1-1**: compare six database careers and the course topics each one needs.

## 2. Choose one role

Choose **one** of the six roles from Lesson 1-1:

| Role | Main responsibility (from Lesson 1-1) | Key weeks |
|---|---|---|
| Database administrator (DBA) | Keeps databases available, fast, secure and recoverable | 1–3, 9 |
| SQL / database developer | Designs schemas and writes queries, views and stored procedures in an application team | 1–3, 7 |
| Data architect | Designs data models, sets standards and governance, chooses data platforms | 4–7, 9 |

Job titles vary. An advert for a "database reliability engineer" may describe DBA work; an "analytics engineer" may be a data engineer or a BI analyst. Choose adverts by their **responsibilities**, not by their titles, and say in your report why each advert belongs to your chosen role.

## 3. Find three real job adverts

Find **three real job adverts** for your chosen role, **published in the last six months**.

- They may come from any country, any company and any job site (company career pages, LinkedIn, national job portals, and so on).
- They must be real, current adverts. Invented, generic or template adverts score 0 for evidence.
- Save a copy (PDF or screenshot) of each advert when you find it, because adverts are often removed after the position is filled. Attach the three copies as an appendix.
- Prefer adverts that list concrete requirements. An advert with a single line of text gives you little to analyse.

### How to cite each advert

List the adverts at the end of the report, numbered A1, A2 and A3, in this format:

> **A1.** *Job title* – Company, City/Country. Published [date] on [site]. Accessed [date]. [URL]

Example (invented, for format only):

> **A1.** *Senior Data Engineer* – Example Retail Ltd, Skopje, North Macedonia. Published 2 September 2026 on example-jobs.com. Accessed 20 October 2026. https://example-jobs.com/12345

In the text, refer to the adverts by number, for example "All three adverts ask for Apache Spark (A1, A2, A3)". When you quote a requirement, use quotation marks and the advert number: "experience with high availability and disaster recovery" (A2).

## 4. What to write (600–800 words)

Use these headings in your report.

### 4.1 The role and the adverts (about 100 words)

Name the role you chose and introduce the three adverts in a sentence each: company type, country, seniority. Explain briefly why each advert belongs to this role, especially if its title is different.

### 4.2 Daily responsibilities (about 150 words)

Summarise what a person in this role actually does, **across the three adverts**. Do not copy the list from one advert. Group similar responsibilities, and note any that appear in only one advert.

### 4.3 Skills and certifications (about 150–200 words)

Report the technical skills, soft skills and certifications that the adverts ask for. Make clear which ones appear in **all three**, which in two, and which in only one. A small table is welcome:

| Requirement | A1 | A2 | A3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| PostgreSQL | ✓ | ✓ | |
| Backup and recovery | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| … | | | |

(A table counts towards the word limit only for its text, not its ticks.)

### 4.4 Where CE 502 appears in this role (about 150 words)

Map the requirements to the weeks of CE 502. For example, an advert that mentions "high availability", "disaster recovery" or "query tuning" is asking for the content of Weeks 1 to 3; one that mentions Spark, a data warehouse or dimensional modelling is asking for Weeks 7 and 8; one that mentions embeddings, pgvector or RAG is asking for Weeks 10 to 12. Name at least **three** links, each tied to a specific advert.

### 4.5 My 12-month learning plan (about 150 words)

Write a personal plan for the next twelve months with **at least three concrete steps**, each with an approximate time and a reason taken from your evidence. Examples of steps: a certification, an online course, a project you will build, a contribution to an open-source project, an internship. Explain how each step closes a gap between your current skills and the adverts.

Example: "All three adverts ask for Apache Spark (A1–A3), which I have never used. Months 3–5: complete a Spark course and build a small pipeline that loads a public dataset into a warehouse, as preparation for CE 502 Weeks 7 and 8."

### 4.6 References and appendix

- The list of adverts A1–A3 in the format of section 3.
- Any other sources you used (for example a salary survey or a certification page), with links.
- Appendix: a copy of each advert (PDF or screenshot). The appendix does not count towards the word limit or the page count.

## 7. Rubric (20 points)

Each criterion is worth 4 points.

| Criterion | 4 – Excellent | 3 – Good | 2 – Adequate | 0 – Missing or inadequate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Evidence: three cited adverts** | Three real adverts for the role, all published in the last six months, fully cited (company, country, date, link) and attached; requirements quoted accurately. | Three real, recent adverts; one citation incomplete or one copy missing. | Only two usable adverts, or adverts older than six months, or citations missing key details. | Fewer than two real adverts, invented or generic adverts, or no citations. |
| **Role description** | A clear, synthesised description of daily responsibilities across all three adverts, with differences noted; explains why each advert belongs to the role. | Accurate description drawn from the adverts, but little synthesis or comparison. | Description mostly copied from one advert, or partly from general knowledge. | Role described from memory, not from the adverts, or a different role than chosen. |
| **Skills and certifications** | Technical skills, soft skills and certifications identified, with clear evidence of which appear in all, some or one advert. | Main skills and certifications identified with advert references; frequency only partly shown. | A list of skills without advert references or without certifications. | Skills missing, invented or unrelated to the adverts. |
| **Link to CE 502 topics** | At least three specific, correct links between advert requirements and named weeks of CE 502, each tied to an advert. | Two or three correct links, or links that are correct but not tied to specific adverts. | One correct link, or links that are vague ("the course is useful"). | No link to the course, or incorrect links. |
| **Learning plan and writing** | A realistic 12-month plan with at least three concrete, dated steps justified by the evidence; clear, well-organised writing within 600–800 words. | A plan with three steps, but weakly justified or not dated; writing mostly clear. | A plan with fewer than three steps or generic steps ("learn more SQL"); writing hard to follow in places or outside the word range. | No plan, or writing that prevents understanding. |

- Copying from other students, from earlier years or from websites is treated under the university's academic integrity regulations.

---

## 9. Common mistakes to avoid

- **Describing a role from memory.** This is the most common weakness. Reports written that way are vague and often out of date. Quote the requirements the adverts actually list.
- **Choosing adverts by title only.** A "Data Analyst" advert may describe a data engineer's work. Read the responsibilities.
- **Listing skills without frequency.** The point of three adverts is comparison: a skill in all three is a much stronger signal than a skill in one.
- **A plan with no evidence.** Every step of your plan should answer a gap you found in the adverts.
- **Losing the evidence.** Save each advert as PDF when you find it.
